Understanding API Gravity

In the petroleum industry, the density of crude oil is usually expressed in terms of API gravity. It is a measure of how heavy or light a petroleum liquid is compared to water.

  • > 31.1° Light crude oil: Flows freely and yields more valuable products like gasoline.
  • 22.3° to 31.1° Medium crude oil.
  • < 22.3° Heavy crude oil: Highly viscous, requiring more processing to yield high-value products.
The formula to convert API Gravity to specific gravity (SG) at 60°F is:
$$SG = \frac{141.5}{\text{API} + 131.5}$$

The Formula Triad: Density, Mass, and Volume

Our tool uses the fundamental relationship between density (\(\rho\)), mass (\(m\)), and volume (\(V\)):

Finding Density

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}$$

Finding Mass

$$m = \rho \times V$$

Finding Volume

$$V = \frac{m}{\rho}$$

Procedure of Calculation: Step-by-Step Example

Real-Life Scenario: Finding Total Mass

Problem: A storage tank holds exactly 10,000 Barrels (bbl) of Crude Oil. The density of the oil is measured at 850 kg/m³. What is the total mass of the oil in Metric Tons?

  1. Convert Volume to a Compatible Unit (m³):
    Since density is in kg/m³, we must convert Barrels to cubic meters. The standard conversion is \(1 \text{ bbl} \approx 0.158987 \text{ m}^3\).
    \(V = 10,000 \times 0.158987\) = 1,589.87 m³
  2. Apply the Mass Formula:
    \(m = \rho \times V\)
    \(m = 850 \text{ kg/m}^3 \times 1,589.87 \text{ m}^3\) = 1,351,389.5 kg
  3. Convert Mass to Final Target Unit (Metric Tons):
    There are \(1,000 \text{ kg}\) in a Metric Ton.
    \(m = 1,351,389.5 \div 1,000\) = 1,351.39 Metric Tons

Temperature Corrections

Crude oil expands when heated and contracts when cooled. Because density and volume are temperature-dependent, the industry standardizes measurements to exactly **60°F (15.56°C)**.

What is a Barrel (bbl)?

In the oil industry, a barrel is the standard unit of volume. Historically, oil was transported in 42-gallon tierces (a type of wooden cask) derived from the Pennsylvania oil rush of the 1860s. Today, the standard stands globally:

  • 1 Oil Barrel (bbl) = 42 US Gallons
  • 1 Oil Barrel (bbl) ≈ 158.987 Liters
  • 1 Oil Barrel (bbl) ≈ 34.97 UK Gallons
  • 1 Oil Barrel (bbl) ≈ 0.1589 Cubic Meters (m³)

Because different commodities are priced in different units (e.g., Crude Oil in Barrels, Natural Gas in MMBtu), it is critical to use our Volume Engine to properly convert your physical measurements into the specific unit required by the commodity market before applying the price valuation.
