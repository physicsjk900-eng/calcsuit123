The EMI Calculation Formula:
Deciphering Loan Math

Published May 19, 2026 • 7 min read

Whether you are purchasing a car, securing a home mortgage, or budgeting personal expenses, understanding how your monthly payments are calculated is vital. In financial lending, this fixed payment is known as the **Equated Monthly Installment (EMI)**.

An EMI represents a structured payment plan consisting of both **principal** (repaying the original borrowed sum) and **interest** (the fee charged by the lender). Over the life of the loan, the ratio of interest to principal inside each payment shifts—a process called **amortization**.

1. The Reducing Balance EMI Formula

Most retail loans (housing, car, personal) utilize a **reducing balance method**. In this system, interest is calculated only on the remaining unpaid principal balance at the end of each month.

The algebraic formula to compute reducing balance EMIs is:

$$EMI = P \times r \times \frac{(1 + r)^n}{(1 + r)^n - 1}$$
Variable Glossary:
  • \(P\): Principal loan amount borrowed.
  • \(r\): Monthly interest rate. Calculated as: \(\frac{\text{Annual Rate}}{12 \times 100}\). (For example, an annual rate of 12% means \(r = \frac{12}{1200} = 0.01\)).
  • \(n\): Loan tenure in months. (For example, a 5-year loan tenure means \(n = 5 \times 12 = 60\) payments).

2. Step-by-Step EMI Calculation Example

Let's calculate the EMI for a loan with the following parameters:

  • Principal Loan Amount (\(P\)) = $10,000
  • Annual Interest Rate = 12% (so monthly rate \(r = 0.01\))
  • Loan Tenure = 12 months (\(n = 12\))

Mathematical Walkthrough:

  • Step 1: Calculate \((1+r)^n\): \((1.01)^{12} \approx 1.1268\)
  • Step 2: Solve the division factor: \(\frac{1.1268}{1.1268 - 1} = \frac{1.1268}{0.1268} \approx 8.8864\)
  • Step 3: Multiply by Principal and Monthly Rate: \(EMI = 10000 \times 0.01 \times 8.8864 = 888.48\)
  • Result: Your Equated Monthly Installment is **$888.49** per month.
  • Total Repayment: over 12 months, you pay \(12 \times \$888.49 = \$10,661.88\) (Interest cost is \$661.88).

3. Flat Interest vs. Reducing Balance Rates

Be cautious of lenders offering **flat interest rates**. In a flat interest loan, interest is calculated on the full principal value for the entire loan duration, ignoring any principal you pay off:

$$EMI\ (Flat) = \frac{P + (P \times \text{Annual Rate} \times \text{Years})}{n}$$

A flat interest rate of 8% usually leads to much higher total interest costs than a reducing rate of 8%, because reducing balance accounts for your monthly principal paydowns. Always confirm which model your bank is using!
