The "Average" Mystery

Most people use the word "average" to mean the Arithmetic Mean. However, in statistics, "average" is an umbrella term for Central Tendency—the middle or typical value of a data set.

Our calculator provides the mean, median, and range because one number rarely tells the whole story. For instance, the "average" salary in a room might be $50,000, but if a billionaire walks in, the mean jumps to $1,000,000 while the median stays exactly where it was.

Concept Check: The Mean

"If 5 friends have 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 dollars, what is the mean?"

1. Sum: 10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50 = 150

2. Count: 5

3. Divide: 150 / 5 = 30

The Mean is $30.

Pro Hack: The Outlier Trap

Always compare your Mean to your Median.

  • Mean > Median: Your data is "Right Skewed" (high outliers).
  • Mean < Median: Your data is "Left Skewed" (low outliers).
  • Equal: You have a "Perfect Bell Curve."
