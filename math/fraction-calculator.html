Study Guide & Manual

Master the concepts behind fractional geometry and addition.

Fractions represent a part of a whole or, more generally, any number of equal parts. When spoken in everyday English, a fraction describes how many parts of a certain size there are, for example, one-half, eight-fifths, three-quarters.

The Components:

  • Numerator (Top Number): Indicates how many parts are selected.
  • Denominator (Bottom Number): Indicates how many total parts equal a whole object. It cannot be zero.
  • Fraction Bar (Vinculum): The line separating the two figures, mathematically signifying division.

To add or subtract fractions, they must share the same denominator (a Common Denominator). You cannot add thirds to quarters directly!

The Process:

Find the easiest common denominator by multiplying the two denominators together (e.g., 3 and 4 becomes 12). Then scale the numerators accordingly.

Example: 1/2 + 2/3
Convert: (1×3)/(2×3) + (2×2)/(3×2)
= 3/6 + 4/6
= 7/6

Multiplication:

Unlike addition, you DO NOT need common denominators. Just multiply straight across: Numerator × Numerator and Denominator × Denominator.

Division (Keep-Change-Flip):

When dividing fractions, remember the rhyme "Keep, Change, Flip." Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and flip the second fraction (use its reciprocal).

1/2 ÷ 3/4 = 1/2 × 4/3 = 4/6 = 2/3

Complete Guide to Fraction Algebra

Fractions are the backbone of algebraic thinking. Whether you are solving for \(x\) or calculating probabilities, mastering fractional arithmetic is essential. This guide breaks down the four core operations of fraction algebra with clear examples and formulas.

1. Addition and Subtraction: The Common Denominator

To add or subtract fractions, the "units" (the denominators) must be identical. If they are not, we find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators.

$$\frac{a}{b} \pm \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad \pm bc}{bd}$$

Example:

Solve: $$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{5}$$
Step 1: Find common denominator ($$3 \times 5 = 15$$).
Step 2: $$\frac{1 \times 5}{15} + \frac{2 \times 3}{15} = \frac{5}{15} + \frac{6}{15}$$
Result: $$\frac{11}{15}$$

2. Multiplication: The Direct Approach

Multiplication is the most straightforward operation in fraction algebra. Unlike addition, you do not need a common denominator. You simply multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}$$

Example:

Solve: $$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{7}$$
Step 1: Multiply across: $$\frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 7} = \frac{6}{28}$$
Step 2: Simplify by dividing by 2: $$\frac{3}{14}$$
Result: $$\frac{3}{14}$$

3. Division: The "Keep-Change-Flip" Rule

Division of fractions is actually multiplication in disguise. To divide by a fraction, you multiply by its reciprocal.

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc}$$

Example:

Solve: $$\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{3}{4}$$
Step 1: Keep $$\frac{1}{2}$$ Change $$\div$$ to $$\times$$ Flip $$\frac{3}{4} ~~~to~~~ \frac{4}{3}$$
Step 2: $$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{3} = \frac{4}{6}$$
Result: $$\frac{2}{3}$$

4. Simplification & Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)

A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factors other than 1. This is achieved by dividing both sides by the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD).

To simplify $$\frac{24}{36}$$
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36
Divide both by 12: $$\frac{24 \div 12}{36 \div 12} = \frac{2}{3}$$
